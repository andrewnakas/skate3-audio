# sub_82B3D668

- Role: `int f(stream*, uint32 request)` — grow the stream's float ring. Same object as the
  `sub_82B3DB90` / `sub_82B3DC48` / `sub_82B3DD90` / `sub_82B3DEA8` / `sub_82B3DF90` family, and
  this is the function that fixes its units: the ring at `+0` is `u32[+48]` consecutive
  per-channel spans of `u32[+20]` words. 284 lifted lines, two direct callees
  (`sub_82B3D590` and `sub_82EDF460`, the XDK memcpy the rest of the family also uses), two own
  `bctrl`, no imports, no timebase, no lock. 18 boot calls on `RwAudioCore Dac` (tier D).
- Three paths. (a) `u32[+0] == 0`: tail-call `sub_82B3D590(self, u32[+48], request, u32[+16])`
  and return its r3 — nothing else happens. (b) new span = `((request+32) & ~31) + u32[+24]`;
  if `(int32)u32[+20] >= new span` only `+12` is written and the result is 1. (c) otherwise
  allocate `channels * new_span` words, move each channel, free the old ring, publish.
- The per-channel move, three memcpys, all in bytes (`rlwinm ...,2,0,29`):
  `old_base = ring + span*c*4`; `src = old_base + ((position mod span) + lag)*4 - u32[+36]*4`,
  wrapped forward by `(span-lag)*4` when it falls outside `[old_base, old_base+span*4)`;
  `dst_end = new_base + (new_span-lag)*4`; `run = min(u32[+36], (old_base+(span-lag)*4 - src)/4)`.
  Then `memcpy(dst_end - u32[+36]*4, src, run*4)`, `memcpy(dst_end - (u32[+36]-run)*4, old_base,
  (u32[+36]-run)*4)` and `memcpy(new_base, dst_end, lag*4)`. `+20`, `+24`, `+36`, `+48`, `+52`
  and `+0` are all **reloaded every iteration** and `+36`/`+24`/`+48` again between the memcpys.
- Stores (four, plus the `stwu` back chain): `stw r21,0(r31)` the new ring base,
  `stw r23,20(r31)` the new span, `stw r11,52(r31)` the position reset to `u32[+24]` (reloaded,
  not reused), all on path (c) only; `stw r19,12(r31)` the request, on (b) and (c). Four bytes
  each. `__savegprlr_18`'s r18..r31 + lr spills and the back chain are this call's own frame.
- Constants, both computed as `((imm & 0xFFFF) << 16) + offset`: the System pointer
  `0x83070000 + 30252 = 0x8307762C` (port_82B1E458's `kSystemPtr`), and the allocation tag
  `0x820F0000 - 10732 = 0x820ED614`, which is passed in r5 **sign-extended** into the high word
  (`lis` + `addi` on a 64-bit register) — reproduced as such.
- Window rationale: `Windows()` returns **false**. Even setting gate 1 aside, the three memcpy
  destinations are inside a freshly allocated block whose address does not exist before the call,
  so the write set is not enumerable from entry state either — gate 2 fails as well.
- Result: r3 is 1 on (b) and (c), 0 on a failed allocation, and `sub_82B3D590`'s own r3 on (a).
  `kReturnR3` is the honest mask here as well as the lint-required one.
- Gate 1 **fails at depth 0**, twice: `bctrl` at **0x82B3D6F8** — `allocate(bytes, 0x820ED614, 0,
  128)` through the System allocator's vtable slot `+4` — and **0x82B3D838**, the old ring's free
  through slot `+12`, the same entry `sub_82B32870` and `sub_82B48F28` use. `sub_82B2F150`'s
  header already records the first of these, one level up (it reads r7=128 as the size; it is the
  alignment, r4 is the size).
- Unsure, and worth reading before trusting the role line: the *placement* does not reconcile with
  the new position. The carried-over words land ending at `new_span - lag`, while the published
  position is `lag`, whose read cursor is `2*lag`. That is consistent with a reader that walks
  **backwards** from the cursor through the mirrored `[0, lag)` window and continues at
  `new_span - lag - 1`, which is exactly where the copy ends — but that reading is inferred from
  the arithmetic alone and was not checked against a reader. The third memcpy also copies
  `[new_span-lag, new_span)`, which this call never writes, into `[0, lag)`; under the mirror
  reading that is the invariant being maintained rather than a bug, but it is unconfirmed.
  `+16` is only passed on to `sub_82B3D590` and is named from `sub_82B3DD90`'s `kObjBlock`.
