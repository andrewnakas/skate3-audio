# sub_82B269C0

Non-leaf, 109 lines, 75,191 calls per boot. f1 = an angle, f2 = a depth, r3 = the panner.

Scales the angle by the constant at 0x822F8A6C, takes its cosine and sine through
`sub_82F4DFB0` and `sub_82F4DED0`, multiplies both by the depth, and hands the pair to the
already-verified `sub_82B453D8`, which clamps it into the unit disc and stores x, y and the
squared length at +0/+4/+8. It then stores the scaled angle at +12, biased by the constant at
0x82060C44 when the depth is not positive -- a NaN depth takes the bias path, since the compare
is unordered.

Write set: one span `{r3, 16}` -- the callee's three floats plus this function's angle word.
`kReturnNone`.

Gate 1 holds because of what the callees do NOT do. Both math routines write only their own red
zone (two and four `stfd` at negative r1 offsets), with no guest state, no indirect calls and no
timebase, so replaying them against rewound memory is sound and they need no windows.
`sub_82B453D8` is a leaf already verified in this loop.

The one thing a port must not skip: the original spills **f29, f30, f31 and r31** into its red
zone and restores them at the end, because its callees may clobber the volatile file. The
harness compares f14-f31, so a native body that simply left those registers alone would be
wrong whenever a callee touched them. This port saves and restores them through locals, which
is the same observable behaviour without writing the stack.
